Hundred-Syllable Mantra

The hundred-syllable mantra is the long mantra of Vajrasattva, recited in the Tibetan tradition to purify harmful actions and broken commitments. It is known by that count, although the exact number of syllables varies slightly from edition to edition.

What it says

It opens with om vajrasattva samayam anupālaya, “Om, Vajrasattva, keep the commitment”, and continues as a series of requests: remain close to me, be steadfast toward me, be pleased with me, grant me every accomplishment, make my mind good in all actions. It closes with the syllable hūṃ, the sequence ha ha ha ha hoḥ, and a plea to all the tathāgatas: do not abandon me.

How it is practiced

The recitation is the core of Vajrasattva meditation in the ngöndro, the preliminary practices. The practitioner visualizes Vajrasattva above the crown of the head, recites the mantra and imagines a stream of white nectar flowing down through the body, washing out harm and obscurations. Many practitioners recite it a hundred thousand times as part of the preliminaries.

What actually does the work

Tibetan teachers insist that the mantra works inside the four opponent powers: reliance, regret, the remedy and resolve. Recited without regret and without resolve, it becomes a formula. Recited with both, it becomes a way of closing an account.

As a method

As a method, the hundred syllables give guilt a shape and an ending: a clear sequence to follow, instead of a scene replayed with no end in sight.
