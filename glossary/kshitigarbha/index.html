Kshitigarbha

Kshitigarbha (Sanskrit: क्षितिगर्भ, Kṣitigarbha, “earth store” or “womb of the earth”; Tibetan: ས་ཡི་སྙིང་པོ, Sa'i Nyingpo; Chinese: Dizang; Japanese: Jizō) is the bodhisattva tied to those in the worst states of suffering, to the dead, and to the people who grieve for them.

The vow

He is known for one of the most radical vows in Mahayana literature, usually summed up as: until the hells are empty, I will not become a Buddha. The Sutra of the Fundamental Vows of Kshitigarbha, preserved in Chinese and traditionally attributed to the translator Śikṣānanda in the Tang dynasty, tells his previous lives, including one as a young woman who searched the hells for her dead mother.

The image

Most bodhisattvas wear princely jewels. Kshitigarbha appears as a monk with a shaved head, holding a staff with six rings, said to open the doors of the hells, and a wish-fulfilling jewel that lights up the dark. In Tibet he is one of the Eight Great Bodhisattvas. In Japan, as Jizō, he became the protector of children and travelers, and small stone Jizō statues line roads and temple grounds, often dressed in red bibs by grieving parents.

As a method

Read as a method, Kshitigarbha is the figure for grief: someone who goes down to where the pain is and stays there, without asking it to hurry. That includes the losses nobody holds a funeral for.
