What karma really means

Karma is not an entity with a notebook logging your sins. It's cause and effect, and cause and effect can be worked with.

What karma is not

Few words have been treated as badly as this one. It became an internet joke, a veiled threat, a consolation for people waiting for the universe to send someone else the bill. None of those has much to do with what the tradition actually describes.

Karma isn't destiny and it isn't punishment. Read that way, it becomes an excuse: for passivity, for cruelty, for waiting. The real idea is smaller and far more useful.

Consequence you can manage

In the traditional description, karma is intentional action and its results. Its advantage over fate is enormous: fate can only be accepted, but consequence can be steered. What you plant today largely shapes the field of choices available tomorrow. The work is spotting the patterns you keep feeding, and interrupting a cycle before it closes again.

A practice for a repeating pattern

When you notice the same result arriving again.

  1. Trace it back to an action. Not to fate, but to a specific thing you did or keep doing. Consequence starts somewhere you can see.
  2. Find the seed. What intention keeps planting this? Name the pattern you're feeding, honestly.
  3. Change one input. You can't control the harvest directly, only what you plant. Pick one different action this time.
  4. Leave other people's karma alone. Someone else's consequences are not your job to collect. Tend your own field.
