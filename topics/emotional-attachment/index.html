Attachment vs. genuine love

Attachment and care look almost identical from the outside and run in opposite directions from the inside. Most of the suffering is in mistaking one for the other.

Why "just let go" is useless advice

Nearly every teaching about attachment ends in the same place: let go. The trouble is nobody explains how you release something the mind has filed as part of itself. So the instruction evaporates on contact, and what's left is guilt for failing to follow it.

Attachment isn't only about people. It's how the mind grips outcomes, identities and certainties, and refuses to open its hand even when the gripping is what hurts.

The line between holding and caring

Avalokiteshvara works on the border between attachment and genuine care: two movements that look alike and operate in reverse. Care wants the other's good and can survive not getting a guarantee. Attachment wants the anxiety to stop and demands the guarantee to get there. The practice is learning to feel the difference in the moment, and to choose the one that doesn't squeeze.

A practice for the grip

When you notice the mind fused to an outcome or a person.

  1. Catch the squeeze. Notice the physical grip (the checking, the waiting for a reply, the rehearsed conversation). Name it as gripping.
  2. Ask the honest question. "Do I want their good, or do I want my anxiety to stop?" Both are human. Only one is care.
  3. Loosen one finger. Choose a single act of not-gripping: don't send the message, don't check, let the silence be silence.
  4. Return to what's real. Put attention on what's actually in front of you now, not the outcome you're trying to force.
