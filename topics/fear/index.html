How to act despite fear

There's a real difference between feeling fear and being run by it. The whole method turns on that gap.

Courage is not the absence of fear

We're taught to wait for confidence before acting, expecting the fear to clear first, then move. For most decisions that matter, it never clears in time. So the waiting becomes the decision, and the fear wins by default.

The tradition reverses the order. It treats fear as a state you act inside, not a gate you wait to open. The question stops being "how do I stop being afraid?" and becomes "what do I do with my hands and feet while the fear is here?"

The figure with a foot already stepping down

Tara is the one major figure in the iconography shown with a foot outside the meditation posture: mid-step, about to move. That posture is the instruction. Stillness that acts. The practice trains the move from contraction to action directly, without requiring calm as a precondition.

A practice for the moment of fear

Under five minutes, usable right before the thing you're avoiding.

  1. Separate the fear from the threat. Ask: is something happening now, or am I rehearsing what might? Name which one it is.
  2. Breathe to the body. Three slow breaths, exhale longer than the inhale: enough to unlock the freeze, not to erase the fear.
  3. Choose the one next action. Not the whole thing. The single next physical step you can take in the next minute.
  4. Step while afraid. Take that action without waiting to feel ready. Readiness, if it comes, comes after the step.
