From the book: Avalokiteshvara

The jewel in the lotus — the Tibetan method to love without losing yourself and to turn attachment into compassion.

“Tradition tells us that there was once a time when the great Bodhisattva of compassion looked upon the suffering of the world and made an immense vow: he would not rest, he would not enter final peace, as long as even one being remained trapped in pain. And so he worked. He crossed the realms of suffering one by one, liberating countless beings, emptying the ocean of anguish with his own hands.

Then, one day, he looked back. And he saw that the ocean was full again. No matter how many beings he had saved, more kept arriving, suffering, endlessly. The weight of it — infinite compassion facing infinite suffering — was too great. And his body, tradition says, shattered into a thousand pieces.

It was not weakness. It was an excess of love without a channel through which to flow. It was the heart trying to hold the entire world in its arms and discovering, in the worst possible way, that arms that tighten around what is too vast end up breaking.”
