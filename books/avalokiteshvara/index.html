About the book

Attachment isn't only about people. It's about how the mind grips outcomes, identities and certainties — and refuses to let go.

Almost every piece of advice about attachment ends in the same place: let go. The trouble is that nobody explains how you release something the mind counts as part of itself. So the instruction evaporates in practice and what remains is guilt for failing to apply it.

Avalokiteshvara starts somewhere else. It maps a practical path for recognizing the grasping pattern in real time — mid-conversation, mid-wait for a reply, mid-decision that depends on someone else — and for responding with something more useful than resignation or control: compassion as a tool, not as a passive feeling.

The book works above all on the border between attachment and genuine care. They are two movements that look alike from the outside and operate in opposite ways from the inside: one tightens, the other holds. Showing where that line usually gets lost — in relationships, in projects, in the picture you keep of yourself — and how to recover it is the core work of this volume.

The figure of Avalokiteshvara enters here as method, not devotion. Tradition says his body shattered before the infinite suffering of the world: not from weakness, but from an excess of love with no channel to run through. It is an accurate description of what happens to anyone who cares without limit and calls it love.

Frequently asked questions

Does this book teach you to detach from people?

No. It works on the difference between attachment and care. The point is not to feel less, but to stop squeezing what cannot be held by squeezing.

Do I need to read Tara first?

No. Each volume in the series stands alone. If attachment, relationship or loss is what weighs most right now, this is the natural entry point.

Is it useful when a relationship is ending?

Yes. A large part of the book deals precisely with the period in which the mind keeps talking to someone who is no longer there.

About the author

Hugo Salles is a writer, lawyer and executive. For over twenty years he has led teams and projects — an experience that brought his study of Mahayana and Tibetan Buddhism into contact with the concrete problems of everyday life. He has translated selected Buddhist sutras into Portuguese and writes The Technologies of the Awakened Mind.
