From the book: Vajrasattva

The crystal that purifies — the Tibetan method to free yourself from guilt without denying the mistake.

"There is, in the Tibetan tradition, a figure that carries no weapon. It raises no sword, wields no thunderbolt, bares no fangs. It is white, white as milk, as snow, as the first light before any color has decided the day. It sits, serene, and in its hands it holds two small objects: a scepter against the chest and a bell at the hip. Nothing in it threatens. Nothing in it accuses. And that is precisely why it is the hardest figure to understand for anyone who arrives carrying guilt.

Because a person carrying guilt is not looking for serenity. They are looking for a sentence. Deep down, the person who feels guilty wants a courtroom, wants someone to confirm the verdict they have already signed against themselves. They want to be condemned with the same severity they use on themselves on the inside. And this white figure refuses the role of judge. It did not come to condemn, nor to absolve for free. It came to do something almost no one has learned to do with their own past: to wash without erasing."
