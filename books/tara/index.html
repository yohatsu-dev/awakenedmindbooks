About the book

There is a difference between understanding anxiety and knowing what to do with it the moment it arrives. This book is about the second thing.

Books about anxiety usually explain the mechanism: what happens in the body, why the mind rehearses the worst, how fear settles in. The explanation helps, but it arrives late. It is of little use at two in the morning, or five minutes before a difficult conversation, or in the instant when a decision has to be made and the body locks up.

Tara treats the Buddhist figure as what it has always been within the Tibetan tradition: a method of action in the face of fear, not a symbol to contemplate from a distance. In the iconography, Tara is the one major figure depicted with a foot outside the meditation posture — ready to step down, ready to act. That is the thesis of the entire book. Courage here is not the absence of fear: it is what you do with fear while it is still present.

The book reconstructs practices drawn from centuries of contemplative tradition and translates them into applicable steps: what to do in the middle of an anxiety crisis, how to break paralysis before a hard decision, how to interrupt the loop of anticipation that turns a small problem into a permanent threat, how to act with fear present instead of waiting for it to pass.

No promise of instant healing, no requirement of belief. The method is described so that it can be tested, repeated and judged by the reader's own experience — which is, after all, the criterion the tradition itself always proposed.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to be a Buddhist to read Tara?

No. The book presents the practice as a method, in plain language, and asks for no adherence to any belief. Traditional elements are explained as they appear, with no prior knowledge required.

Is Tara a meditation book?

Not exactly. There are practices, some of them contemplative, but the focus is what to do the moment fear shows up — including outside any formal meditation context.

In what order should I read The Technologies of the Awakened Mind?

Each volume stands on its own and addresses a specific mental pattern. Tara is the first in the series and a good starting point for readers dealing with fear and anxiety, but you can begin with whichever theme weighs most right now.

About the author

Hugo Salles is a writer, lawyer and executive. For over twenty years he has led teams and projects — an experience that brought his study of Mahayana and Tibetan Buddhism into contact with the concrete problems of everyday life. He has translated selected Buddhist sutras into Portuguese and writes The Technologies of the Awakened Mind.
